An order defines how and at what price you want to buy or sell a perpetual contract.

6MM provides multiple order types to support different trading strategies and risk preferences.

Understanding order types helps you enter and exit positions more effectively.

***

<span data-sixmm-canonical-anchor="market-order" aria-hidden="true"></span>

## Market Order

A market order seeks execution against available liquidity at the best available prices. Execution depends on order-book depth and trading restrictions: the order may fill at multiple prices, fill partially, or fail to complete. Check the actual order status and fills.

***

<span data-sixmm-canonical-anchor="limit-order" aria-hidden="true"></span>

## Limit Order

A Limit Order executes only at the specified price or better.

Key features:

* Full price control
* May not be filled immediately
* Can rest in the order book

Best for:

* Entering at a specific price
* Reducing slippage
* Providing liquidity

***

<span data-sixmm-canonical-anchor="stop-order" aria-hidden="true"></span>

## Stop Order

A Stop Order is triggered when the market reaches a predefined price.

Once triggered, it becomes either a Market Order or a Limit Order, depending on configuration.

Common use cases:

* Stop Loss
* Breakout entry
* Risk management

***

<span id="take-profit-stop-loss-tp-sl" data-sixmm-canonical-anchor="take-profit-stop-loss-tp-sl" aria-hidden="true"></span>

## Take Profit / Stop Loss (TP / SL)

Take Profit and Stop Loss are conditional exit instructions. Reaching a trigger price activates the configured order; it does not guarantee a fill, an execution price, a profit or a maximum loss.

Take Profit aims to exit after a favorable price move; Stop Loss aims to reduce exposure after an adverse move. Neither guarantees an execution result or prevents liquidation.

***

Submission means an order request has been sent; acceptance, triggering and execution are separate states. A triggered market order depends on available liquidity. A triggered limit order must also satisfy its limit price and may remain unfilled.

<span data-sixmm-canonical-anchor="reduce-only-orders" aria-hidden="true"></span>

## Reduce-Only Orders

A Reduce-Only order ensures that the order can only reduce or close an existing position.

* Prevents accidental position increases
* Commonly used for exits and risk control

***

<span data-sixmm-canonical-anchor="time-in-force-options" aria-hidden="true"></span>

## Time-in-Force Options

Orders may support time-in-force conditions such as:

* GTC (Good Till Cancelled): Remains active until canceled
* IOC (Immediate or Cancel): Executes immediately or cancels unfilled portion
* FOK (Fill or Kill): Executes fully or cancels entirely

Availability depends on order type and partner configuration.

***

<span data-sixmm-canonical-anchor="order-execution-notes" aria-hidden="true"></span>

## Order Execution Notes

* All orders are validated before submission
* Execution prices are affected by market liquidity
* Fees are applied upon execution

Orders that cannot be filled remain open or are canceled according to their settings.

***

<span data-sixmm-canonical-anchor="beginner-tips" aria-hidden="true"></span>

## Beginner Tips

* Use Limit Orders to control entry price
* Use Market Orders only when speed is critical
* Always set Stop Loss to manage downside risk
* Start with small position sizes

***

## Summary

6MM order types are designed to be:

* Flexible for different strategies
* Easy to understand for beginners
* Compatible with institutional trading standards

Choosing the right order type is a key step in effective perpetual contract trading.
